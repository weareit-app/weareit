# /ai: AI can be helpful. AI can be hurtful. AI can be dangerous.

**Writer:** founder, plainspoken. **Target:** about 500 words in three parts, a "How AI is used here" register, selected primary sources.

**Guardrail (atlas):** keep ethical judgments, disputed legal claims and established facts apart. Never imply AI-made material was handcrafted. Aim for thoughtful candor, not an all-good or all-bad argument.

## Opening (1 paragraph)

The three sentences above as the frame. **You:** why you decided to use AI to build It at all, as a solo founder, knowing the objections.

## Helpful (expandable)

- What it makes possible: one person building something that would normally need a team; drafting and checking content at a scale one person couldn't; wider access to knowledge.
- **You:** one concrete example from building It (code, content drafting, research) where it genuinely helped.
- Label: your view, not an established result.

## Hurtful (expandable)

- Training on creators' work without consent or pay; pressure on wages and bargaining power for writers, artists and developers; confident errors.
- **You:** which of these you feel most, and what you're doing about it (for example: disclosing AI art, founder rewriting all copy, sources required for every claim).
- Disputed legal questions (copyright and training) stated as disputed, with who holds which view.

## Dangerous (expandable)

- Misuse, concentration of control over powerful systems, loss of human oversight. Link to `/risks` for the larger picture instead of repeating it.
- **You:** where you draw boundaries for It (for example: AI never decides what is published; no AI reading of people's private picks).
  - Check against the privacy invariants before stating any boundary as a commitment.
