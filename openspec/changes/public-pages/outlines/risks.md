**Guardrail (atlas):** this page is about risks humanity faces, not a corporate risk register. No disaster imagery, countdowns or composite risk scores. Don't imply It solves any of this by existing.

## Opening (1 paragraph)

Some problems don't just make life worse; they shrink the set of futures still open to us. This page covers a few of them: how each one works, what is known, what people disagree about, and what might help.

## Six areas (founder, 2026-10-19)

### 1. Powerful AI without adequate oversight

- Mechanism: capability growing faster than the ability to check, steer or share control of it.
- Uncertainty: timelines and how hard the control problem is; experts disagree widely, so show the disagreement rather than one forecast.
- Responses people propose: evaluation and auditing, international agreements, open research on alignment.
- Link `/ai` for It's own use of AI.

### 2. Concentrated power

- Mechanism: economic and political power reinforcing each other, so fewer people shape the options everyone else has.
- Evidence: one sourced measure (for example a democracy index, or market concentration in a major industry), with its definition.
- Responses: competition policy, campaign finance rules, ownership models (link the societal pains under Power).

### 3. Rising inequality

- Mechanism: when income and wealth pool at the top, most people's choices narrow, and the gap feeds concentrated power (section 2).
- Evidence: one sourced time series (for example the top 1% share of wealth or income), with its definition, country and denominator.
- Uncertainty: measures disagree (income vs wealth, before vs after taxes and transfers); say which one is shown and why.
- Responses people propose, as proposals: tax changes, universal basic income, wage policy, ownership models.

### 4. Climate change

- Mechanism: warming that raises the cost of every other problem and closes off options that can't be won back.
- Evidence: one sourced chart (for example global temperature against a pre-industrial baseline), with its baseline stated.
- Uncertainty: how fast tipping points arrive; the range of warming under different emissions paths.
- Responses: cutting emissions, adaptation, and the coordination that both need (section 5).

### 5. Lack of coordination

- Mechanism: people who would all be better off acting together still don't, because nobody can count on the others (collective-action problems). This sits underneath most of the other five.
- Evidence: an evidence card on a well-documented case, failed or successful (for example how a shared resource was or wasn't managed).
- Our view (label: working hypothesis): better tools for finding people who want the same future could make some coordination easier. This is the closest link to what It is trying to build; say plainly that It doesn't do this yet (link `/roadmap`).

### 6. The breakdown of shared information

- Mechanism: when people can't agree on basic facts, they can't decide anything together.
- Evidence: one sourced measure (for example trust in news or institutions over time).
- Uncertainty: how much is caused by platforms vs longer trends; researchers disagree.

Other candidates from the atlas for later: war, pandemics and biological threats, economic displacement, irreversible loss of options.
