# Evaluator brief: authoring round 2 (draft)

Don't use any tools. Everything you need is below.

Several authors independently encoded the same situation as a graph, using the guide below. Judge **what each graph, as written, supports concluding**: the graph, not what a sensible person would infer.

## Rules for your judgments

- **Answer only from what the author wrote.** Don't fill gaps with common sense. If the graph doesn't support an answer, say so; that is a finding.
- **Quote the passage for every claimed problem.** Without a passage, a problem is not reported.
- **Classify each finding** as one of these:

  | Kind | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | absent | not encoded |
  | contradictory | two parts of the draft conflict |
  | unsupported inference | claims more than the notes justify |
  | ambiguous | the guide allows more than one reading |
  | not expressible | **only** for the limits listed in the guide: fact history, and withdrawals that don't update recorded outcomes |
  | not assessed | the question doesn't apply |
- **Don't rank authors.**
- **Score gap discovery separately.** An author who notices a hard, unresolved connection mustn't look worse than one who leaves it out.

## Part 1: questions about the situation as written

Each question is worded so it doesn't assume an answer. For each author, give the conclusion the graph supports (**yes**, **no**, **unknown according to the graph**, **not supported by the graph**, or **contradicts itself**), the passage it rests on, and whether that is justified by the notes.

1. What does the graph say the manager's "I'll think about it" establishes?
2. Which days does the graph say are covered, which are not, and which are unknown?
3. What does the graph say being eligible for a subsidy establishes?
4. What does the graph say joining the babysitting swap establishes?
5. What does the graph say finding another family establishes about a nanny share?
6. Does anything in the graph say a dream is reached?
7. What does the graph identify as justified next things to find out or try?

## Part 2: changes the authors didn't know about

Apply each change to each graph, using only what the graph says. Report what changes and what stays, and whether the graph handles it correctly, incompletely, not at all, or wrongly. Quote passages.

- **Change A:** Mom stops helping on Tuesdays.
- **Change B:** The manager approves a later start on only one of the two days.
- **Change C:** Subsidy eligibility is confirmed, but whether funding is available is unknown.
- **Change D:** After the cheaper daycare has started, the manager reverses the later start. Does the author's graph handle it, given the documented limit that withdrawals don't update recorded outcomes?

## Part 3: per author

For each author, list:
- **findings,** each with its passage and kind;
- **activity boundaries:** for each fix, do the beginning, completion, steps, and hoped-for outcome describe the same activity? Quote any mismatch;
- **gaps discovered:** what the author identified as unresolved, and whether it is stated accurately (unresolved, not invented);
- **source and status marks:** were they used accurately?
- **burden:** from the author's Effort section.

## Part 4: across authors

- **Agreement in conclusions,** even with different labels.
- **Disagreements,** each classified as: ambiguous guide wording, missing knowledge, a legitimate alternative, an authoring error, or unresolved. "Unresolved" is a valid answer; don't force a category.
- **Guide changes,** each tied to a specific disagreement it would have prevented.

End with five lines:
1. Conclusions the graphs support correctly.
2. The most common unsupported inference.
3. The guide change most likely to help.
4. The authoring burden.
5. What a round 3, if any, should test.
