## Fixes

### Fix 1: Get a childcare subsidy

- **Intended result:** a subsidy is approved and pays part of my childcare cost. (I chose "approved", not "applied", because applying on its own changes nothing I pay.) assumed
- **May help toward:** Costs I can carry; Keep working. stated (the notes raise it for cost)
- **Needs to start:** an employer letter (needs checking); proof of my hours (needs checking). Nothing else blocks starting: checking eligibility is a step, not a start need. assumed
- **Needs to succeed:** meeting the eligibility rules (needs checking); the application being decided. Having the letter and proof doesn't establish approval; the decision still has to be observed. assumed
- **Made of (steps toward, not a checklist):** check whether I qualify; ask my employer for the letter; gather proof of hours; submit the application. After all four, I still have to record the decision. assumed
- **Who takes part:** me (applicant); my manager or employer (writes the letter); the subsidy agency (decides). stated for me and employer; assumed for the agency
- **Outcomes:**
  - Approved (hoped): establishes "a subsidy pays part of my cost" and nothing about which care I use. assumed
  - Refused (unhoped): establishes "not eligible as things stand", not "never eligible". assumed
  - Decision pending (pending): establishes nothing. assumed
  - Subsidy later ends, for example because my hours change (unhoped): withdraws "a subsidy pays part of my cost" from the Approved outcome; keeps the fact that I was once eligible. assumed

### Fix 2: Get a later start two days a week

- **Intended result:** my manager agrees, on the record, that I start later on two named days. assumed (the notes only say she'd think about it)
- **May help toward:** Keep working; Care I can count on. It helps indirectly: its hoped outcome is a start need for Fix 3. assumed
- **Needs to start:** my manager being open to it. stated (she said she'd think about it)
- **Needs to succeed:** the early shift being covered without me on those days (assumed; nothing in the notes); my manager saying yes (stated as the open question).
- **Made of (steps toward, not a checklist):** follow up with my manager; propose which two days; get the answer in writing. Doing these doesn't settle the answer. assumed
- **Who takes part:** me (asks); my manager (decides). stated
- **Outcomes:**
  - Agreed (hoped): establishes "I can arrive after eight on two days a week", nothing about the other two days. assumed
  - Declined (unhoped): establishes "no later start for now". assumed
  - Still thinking (pending): current state. stated
  - Agreement later reversed (unhoped): withdraws "I can arrive after eight on two days" from Agreed. assumed

- **Needs to succeed:** the fee being affordable, with or without Fix 1 (needs checking); him settling there (needs checking). Enrolment doesn't establish either; both have to be observed over weeks. assumed

- **Who takes part:** me (parent); the place (provider); him (attends). stated
- **Outcomes:**

## Questions

- **Two days or four?** She said a later start on two days "would let me use a cheaper place", but she works four days. Either the other two days are covered some other way (mom, swap, nanny share), or she means the cheaper place only for those two days. The graph can't say which, and the start need of Fix 3 changes with the answer.
- **Is "stop worrying" a dream or a result?** It reads as the strongest wish, but it's a feeling that follows from reliable care rather than a state anyone can arrange directly. I made it a dream and named it "Care I can count on"; a stricter reading would drop it.
- **Is Fix 2 a fix or a step inside Fix 3?** It has its own participant, its own outcome, and its outcome is a start need elsewhere, so I kept it separate. The guide doesn't say whether a fix may be a start need of another fix.
- **Does "cheaper" count toward affordability?** The pain is about affordability, but "cheaper than more than half my income" could still be too much. I kept that as needs checking rather than crediting Fix 3 with the cost dream.
- **Combinations.** The realistic path is probably subsidy plus cheaper place, or swap plus Tuesdays. The guide has no way to say "these two together may help" beyond listing the same dream under each.
- **Facts and dreams.** The guide says facts make a dream "likely to matter, or harder for now", but gives no relationship name for it, so I didn't link facts to dreams.
- **Subsidy result.** I chose "approved and paying" over "applied" or "decision received". "Decision received" is more honest about control, but then the hoped outcome is approval and the result is just "a decision", which felt like a loophole.

## Effort

The hardest part was deciding what counts as a fix at all: the manager conversation, the Tuesdays conversation, and the waiting list are each one action with one answer, and it was unclear whether they deserve full blocks or should collapse into steps of something larger. The second difficulty was keeping "needs to succeed" honest when the real success conditions (affordable, he's happy, hours match) are things nobody will know for weeks; nearly every success need ended up "needs checking", which is probably right but makes the fixes look thin. Ambiguous in the guide: whether one fix's outcome may be another fix's start need; whether a dream may be an emotional state; how facts attach to dreams; and the "say how you know" marks, since most relationships are the author's structure rather than anything the notes state, so almost everything is "assumed" and the mark stops discriminating. One pass with no revision took roughly thirty to forty minutes of writing.
