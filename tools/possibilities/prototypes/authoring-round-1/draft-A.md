## Fixes

### 1. Get approved for a childcare subsidy
- Intended result: a subsidy is approved for her. This isn't "apply" and isn't "care is affordable". (assumed framing)
- May help toward: Stop worrying; Keep working. (assumed)
- Needs to start: knowing which body runs the subsidy where she lives. (needs checking)
- Needs to succeed: being eligible (needs checking). An employer letter and proof of hours, only if the scheme really requires them (needs checking; hearsay).
- Made of, steps toward (not a checklist): check eligibility; get the employer letter; gather proof of hours; submit the application; wait for the decision. Even with every step done, the decision still has to be recorded. (assumed)
- Who takes part: she applies (stated); her employer provides the letter (stated as heard); the subsidy office decides (assumed).
- Outcomes:
  - Approved, **hoped**: establishes "has subsidy approval". It does not establish that care is affordable or that a place is found. (assumed)
  - Turned down, **unhoped**: establishes "not approved on this application". It doesn't establish permanent ineligibility. (assumed)
  - Waiting on a decision, **pending**. (assumed)
  - Subsidy ends or isn't renewed, **unhoped**: withdraws "has subsidy approval" (from the approval outcome). Care already paid for stays paid. (assumed)

## Questions

- Is "keep mom's Tuesdays" a fix or a fact? It's an existing arrangement, not something tried. I made it a fix only so that its ending has something to withdraw.
- Does fix 3's dependence on fix 2 belong under "needs to succeed", pointing at another fix's outcome? The guide doesn't say whether a need can be another fix's outcome or must be a fact.
- Withdrawals across fixes: fix 2's "arrangement withdrawn" should knock out fix 3's attendance. Should I write that in fix 2's outcome or in fix 3's? I put it in fix 3, but either seems valid.
- Is "on the waiting list" a fact or a start need? I couldn't tell from the notes whether she's on it.
- Is "He's somewhere he's happy" a dream specific enough to this pain? I kept it because she said it, but it's close to "fits any pain".
- Is there a further dream about not depending on her mom? I left it out because she didn't say it.

## Effort

The hardest part was choosing intended results. Each fix could plausibly be "apply", "get approved", "get a place", or "have the care actually work", and that choice changes every need. Applying the "needs to succeed" test also kept turning up the result itself (for example, manager agreement), so a few fixes honestly have no separate success needs. The guide is ambiguous on four points:
- whether a need can point at another fix's outcome;
- which fix owns a cross-fix withdrawal;
- whether ongoing arrangements are fixes;
- whether a fact like "no family known" is stated or assumed when it's only implied.

The stated/assumed/needs-checking marking was the slowest part, because most relationship lines are reasonable inferences rather than things the notes say. That pushed nearly everything to "assumed". It took roughly 15–20 minutes of careful work. A person would likely need 45–60 minutes.
