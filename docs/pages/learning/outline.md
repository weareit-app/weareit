# /learning: How does It find out it's wrong?

**Writer:** drafted from this outline; founder rewrites and approves. **Target:** a `/research` note of about 500 words built on one real It finding (see `questions.md` Q1), not a survey of how humans and machines learn. The atlas's wider subject (individual and collective learning, conditions that help or frustrate inquiry) waits for more of It's own evidence.

## 1. The question
- It writes possible futures and fixes with AI help, and the founder rates them. How do you tell whether that loop is getting better, or just getting rated well?

## 2. What is known
- Feedback helps learning only under some conditions, and in more than a third of the interventions reviewed it made performance worse (Kluger and DeNisi; Hattie and Timperley).
- Single-loop and double-loop learning: fixing the error versus questioning the rule that produced it (Argyris and Schön).
- Calibration and scoring of forecasts, as a model of learning from outcomes (Tetlock's tournament work).

## 3. Our present view
- Label: **observed in one evaluation round (October 2026), not replicated.** From `docs/research/possibilities/evaluations/generator-2026-10/evaluation.md`: every numeric check passed, but lower-ranked cards were loved about as often as finalists (14 of 16 against 37 of 40), so the AI judges weren't separating good from weaker cards. The founder's notes found problems the ratings didn't measure (fix kinds, "Learn how…" fixes, dreams written as actions).
- The double-loop lesson: the fix was to change what gets measured, not to tune the rating.
- **You:** what you'd tell a reader this taught you; whether the quotes from your audit notes can be public.

## 4. Why it matters
- Each pain page a person reads came out of this loop. Honest measurement is what keeps those pages useful.

## 5. What remains open
- One round, one rater. Whether the structural checks fix the problem is untested.
- **You:** whether other people's ratings belong in the loop, and how, given that contributed content goes to a review queue first.
