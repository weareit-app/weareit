# /society: research note

- **Created:** 2026-10-20. **Evidence confidence:** high on the outside sources (checked 2026-10-20); there is no It research result on this subject yet.

## What It's work touches

- **The atlas's subject:** "plural lives, institutions, prosperity, shared problems, power, social choice and avenues for collective action. Not an imposed policy platform" (§4).
- **Already covered elsewhere:** the societal page ("What could be better for us?") with several futures per pain; `/risks` (concentrated power, inequality, coordination, shared information); `/possibility`, which takes the unemployment → "live well regardless of employment" → universal basic income example, with the guardrail that the explorer "should not funnel visitors toward one predetermined societal answer". A `/society` page would overlap all three.
- **It's own evidence base:** the societal pains source registry (`docs/source/MVP_Societal_Pains_Source_Registry.md`), which keeps apart what people say bothers them, what researchers measure, what people want changed and what experts interpret as a problem.
- **Constraints:** the copy style guide says contested ideas are offered as ideas, naming who holds them. `/principles` must not claim community ownership or governance before it's true (atlas guardrail; public-essays spec "Essay honest status").

## Key primary sources

- Sen, A. (1999). *Development as Freedom.* Knopf / Oxford UP. — Judge development by people's real freedoms (capabilities), not income alone.
- Sen, A. (1999). "The Possibility of Social Choice." *American Economic Review* 89(3): 349–378. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.89.3.349 — Nobel lecture: impossibility results like Arrow's can be avoided when social choice uses richer information, such as comparisons of well-being.
- Arrow, K. J. (1951). *Social Choice and Individual Values.* Wiley; 2nd ed. Yale UP, 1963. — The impossibility theorem.
- Landemore, H. (2020). *Open Democracy: Reinventing Popular Rule for the Twenty-First Century.* Princeton UP. — A democracy built on open bodies of randomly selected citizens.
- Wright, E. O. (2010). *Envisioning Real Utopias.* Verso. — Ways to change institutions, drawing on working examples such as participatory budgeting and Wikipedia.
- Ostrom (1990, 2010), see `docs/pages/coordination/research.md`.
