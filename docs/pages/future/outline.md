# /future: How far could this go, and what would have to be true?

**Writer:** drafted from this outline; founder rewrites and approves (to confirm, `questions.md` Q1). **Target:** about 500 words: a short opening, three labelled horizons of about 100 words each, one "what would have to happen first?" diagram, and an honest ending.

**Guardrail (atlas):** a scenario is neither a prediction nor a product commitment. No dates or probabilities unless a cited source justifies them. No rocketship roadmap and no single utopia. Show failure modes and the chance the approach doesn't work at all.

## Opening (1 paragraph)

This page is about what It and the wider world might become if certain things turn out to be true. Each horizon below says what it would take and what could go wrong. None of it is a promise or a forecast; the label on each part says how far it is from real. **You:** one sentence on why you think it's worth writing down at all.

## Three horizons (each `expandable`, label shown on the heading)

### 1. Useful soon (label: planned engineering, then working hypothesis)
- What it looks like: the explorer, saved picks, your own pains and futures, and a roadmap where interest from real people decides what gets built next (the "self-recursive" idea from the product spec: It is used to improve It, coordinated by hand at first).
- What would have to be true: people find the explorer useful enough to return and to support It; one person can keep it running.
- What could go wrong: too few people, or support doesn't cover costs, and It stays small or stops.
- Signposts a reader could check: the explorer shipped; roadmap items moved by real interest.
- Link `/roadmap` for anything already planned.

### 2. An ambitious system (label: speculative direction)
- What it looks like: It keeps a changing picture of people's goals, skills, resources and permissions, and helps find people whose different goals still lead to the same next step. Capabilities come from many providers and fit together (`/technology`). You can take your history to another provider. The people who use It own and govern it.
- What would have to be true: goals and permissions can be represented well enough to combine; matching works without exposing anyone; an ownership structure that stays accountable exists in law and in practice.
- What could go wrong: matching invades privacy; governance gets captured by a few (The DAO and later DAO studies in `research.md`; Ostrom's commons principles as the counterweight); the system is too complex to trust.
- Signposts: a working account export; a rule changed by members rather than the founder.
- **You:** which of these you'd keep if you could only keep one.

### 3. Distant speculation (label: speculative direction)
- What it looks like: independent systems cooperate reliably, so groups that never met can find jointly possible actions on shared problems (`/risks` §5, lack of coordination).
- What would have to be true: unresolved research in `/research`; institutions willing to take part.
- What could go wrong: it simply doesn't work; or it works and is used to concentrate power (`/risks` §2).
- **You:** the version of this you'd most fear, said plainly.
