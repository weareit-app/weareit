# /future: How far could this go, and what would have to be true?

**Writer:** drafted from this outline; founder rewrites and approves (`questions.md` Q1). **Target:** about 500 words: an opening, three labeled horizons of about 100 words each, one diagram and an honest ending. **Home:** this outline moves into an OpenSpec change when the page is proposed.

**Guardrail (atlas):** a scenario is neither a prediction nor a product commitment. No dates or probabilities unless a cited source justifies them. No rocketship roadmap and no single utopia. Show failure modes and the chance the approach doesn't work at all.

## Opening (1 paragraph)

What It and the wider world might become if certain things turn out to be true. Why it matters: a picture of what's possible changes what people try, so the conditions belong next to the hopes. None of this is a promise or a forecast; each part carries a label. **You:** one sentence on why it's worth writing down at all.

## Our present view (label: working hypothesis)

Tools that help people with different goals find a shared next step could make some coordination easier. Key assumptions: people will say what they want if it stays private, and enough of them will use and support It. Link `/possibility` for the idea itself.

## Three horizons (each `expandable`, label in the heading)

### 1. Useful soon (label: planned engineering)
- What it would look like: the explorer, saved picks, your own pains and futures, and a roadmap showing what's built, being built and considered, with dated changes. Readers can say what they'd use; that informs choices and never commits It to build anything. It is used to improve It, coordinated by hand (product spec §9, `docs/source/We_Are_It_MVP_Product_UX_Spec_v0.1_2026-10-16.md`).
- What would have to be true: people find the explorer useful enough to return and to support It; one person can keep it running.
- What could go wrong: too few people, or support doesn't cover costs, and It stays small or stops.
- Signposts: the explorer is live; roadmap items show dated status changes.

### 2. An ambitious system (label: speculative direction)
- What it would look like: It would keep a changing picture of people's goals, skills, resources and permissions, and help find people whose different goals lead to the same next step. Capabilities from many providers would fit together (`/technology`). You could take your history to another provider. The people who use It would own and govern it. Today: It is one person and an LLC.
- What would have to be true: goals and permissions can be represented well enough to combine; matching works without exposing anyone; an accountable ownership structure exists in law and in practice.
- What could go wrong: matching invades privacy; governance is captured by a few (The DAO and later DAO studies in `research.md`; Ostrom's commons principles as the counterweight); the system is too complex to trust.
- Signposts: a working account export; a rule changed by members rather than the founder.

### 3. Distant speculation (label: speculative direction)
- What it would look like: independent systems cooperate reliably, so groups that never met can find jointly possible actions on shared problems (the "lack of coordination" area on `/risks`).
- What would have to be true: the open research in `/research`; institutions willing to take part.
- What could go wrong: it doesn't work; or it works and is used to concentrate power (the "concentrated power" area on `/risks`).
- **You:** the version of this you'd most fear, said plainly.
