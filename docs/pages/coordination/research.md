## What It's work touches

- **The atlas's question:** "Why shared goals are not necessary for useful cooperation; how different goals, resources, needs and capabilities might lead to jointly possible actions" (§4).
- **`/risks` area 5, "Lack of coordination"** (founder, 2026-10-19), already outlined with a labelled working hypothesis: better tools for finding people who want the same future could make some coordination easier, and It doesn't do this yet (`openspec/changes/public-pages/outlines/risks.md`).
- **The explorer itself:** several dreams under each pain, more than one can be picked, and fixes sit under each dream. Whether fixes repeat across dreams is checkable from It's content alone, with no personal data. [ASSUMPTION: not yet measured]
- **Planned, not built:** "Matching people with goals to people offering help" is listed as *soon* in `docs/spec/features.md`. Any overlap shown between people involves their choices, so the privacy invariants in `AGENTS.md` apply: "Never expose personal selections or their sensitive derivatives in public artifacts by default." The atlas floats threshold-protected aggregate counts (§1, §9), but AGENTS.md has no such exception; any aggregate would need a decision record first (open question).

## Key primary sources

- Olson, M. (1965). *The Logic of Collective Action.* Harvard UP. https://doi.org/10.4159/9780674041660 — Large groups don't act for a shared interest without selective incentives or coercion (free riding).
- Ostrom, E. (1990). *Governing the Commons.* Cambridge UP. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763 — Case studies of long-lasting self-governed commons (meadows, forests, irrigation, fisheries) and the design principles drawn from them.
- Ostrom, E. (2010). "Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems." *American Economic Review* 100(3): 641–672. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641 — Her Nobel lecture.
- Schelling, T. C. (1960). *The Strategy of Conflict.* Harvard UP. — Focal points: people coordinate without talking on options that stand out.
- Axelrod, R. & Hamilton, W. D. (1981). "The Evolution of Cooperation." *Science* 211(4489): 1390–1396. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7466396 (DOI to confirm at doi.org); Axelrod, R. (1984). *The Evolution of Cooperation.* Basic Books. — Reciprocal cooperation among self-interested players in the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma.
- Arrow, K. J. (1951). *Social Choice and Individual Values.* Wiley (Cowles Monograph 12); 2nd ed. Yale UP, 1963. — No ranked voting rule over three or more options meets all of a short list of fairness conditions at once.
- Treat with care: Hong, L. & Page, S. E. (2004), PNAS 101(46): 16385–16389, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403723101, on diverse problem-solving teams, is disputed by Thompson, A. (2014), *Notices of the AMS* 61(9): 1024–1030, https://doi.org/10.1090/noti1163. Cite both or neither.
