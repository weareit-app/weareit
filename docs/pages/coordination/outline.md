# /coordination: Can people cooperate without wanting the same thing?

**Writer:** drafted from this outline; founder rewrites and approves. **Target:** about 500 words, first as a section linked from `/risks` (area 5, "Lack of coordination") and `/possibility`, then a `/research` note (see `questions.md` Q1). **Guardrail:** It doesn't help people coordinate yet; never write as if it does (link `/roadmap`).

## 1. The question
- People who'd all be better off acting together often don't. The atlas's question goes further: must they share a goal at all? Different goals, resources, needs and abilities can still overlap in an action that works for each person.

## 2. What is known
- Collective-action problems: why groups with a shared interest still fail to act (Olson).
- Communities that do manage shared resources well, and the design rules behind them (Ostrom).
- Cooperation between people with different interests, from repeated play (Axelrod) and focal points that let people coordinate without talking (Schelling).
- Limits: no voting rule turns individual rankings into a group ranking that meets every fairness condition at once (Arrow). Say this plainly; it bounds what any tool can promise.

## 3. Our present view
- Label: **working hypothesis.** Showing people the futures and fixes they'd each choose, side by side, could reveal joint actions nobody was looking for. The explorer's many dreams per pain are a first, small test of that idea: different people pick different futures under the same pain.
- **You:** the example that convinced you. A real case where people with different goals found one action that served each.

## 4. Why it matters
- One worked example: two people under the same pain who want different futures, and a fix that sits under both. Hand-authored, labelled as an example.

## 5. What remains open
- Whether showing overlap makes cooperation more likely, or just more visible. No evidence yet.
- Privacy: overlap means comparing people's choices. AGENTS.md: "Never expose personal selections or their sensitive derivatives in public artifacts by default." Whether any aggregate count could ever be shown, and under what conditions, is an open question; changing the invariant would need a decision record.
