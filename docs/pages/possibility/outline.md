## Opening (1 paragraph)

Points to cover, not copy: the first fix people think of is usually the obvious one, and often the only one. This page is about the other futures that appear when you ask what "better" would mean for you, and what could make them reachable.

**You:** a sentence on why this idea is at the center of It for you, or a moment when you saw a future you hadn't considered.

## What is known (2 short paragraphs, sourced)

- People facing a decision list fewer than half of their own relevant options, and prompting them with their goals helps (Siebert and Keeney; Keeney; Nutt). Label: **replicated finding** (`questions.md` Q13). These studies are about decisions in general, not life futures.
- Sen and Nussbaum's capability approach asks what people are actually able to do and be, not only what they have, and warns that people adjust what they want to what seems available ("adaptive preferences"). Describe what they published; no implied endorsement.

## Our present view (label: working hypothesis)

Different goals and situations lead to more futures than people first see. A pain can be the start of several good lives, not one.

## A worked example: unemployment

Start from the pain as the explorer shows it: **Unemployment. "I can't find work."** Show three or four genuinely different futures, for example:

- Finding work that suits me (the obvious one; keep it, and make it concrete).
- Getting by on savings and benefits while I look, without panic.
- Work I chose, even if it isn't a regular job (self-employment, a cooperative).
- **I can live well regardless of employment.** This one reveals the most: a job gives income and also time structure, contact and purpose (Jahoda), and this future asks how to get each another way.

For that last future, show one fix at each level, with the copy guide's status labels:

1. **Real now:** a Task, such as checking unemployment insurance eligibility, linked to a vetted official resource (which one is to be chosen and checked).
2. **Something It could build:** one Feature, described as a proposal.
3. **A change we'd make together:** universal basic income, named plainly as a proposal people disagree about. Evidence card: what the trials found (Finland, OpenResearch in the US, Stockton, Kenya) and what none tested (cost, financing, a whole country, permanence). Name the alternatives people propose: a job guarantee, stronger unemployment insurance, expanded tax credits, a negative income tax.

**You:** which futures to show, and whether UBI is the right example (`questions.md` Q3–Q5).

## Why it matters (1 paragraph)

If the first option is the only one people see, they judge their life by it. Several real futures, honestly labelled, give a person more to choose from.

## What remains open

- Whether showing more futures helps people act or overwhelms them. It hasn't tested this.
- How to tell a missing future from one nobody wants.
- Whether "possibility" can be described precisely: a research question (link `/research`), not a claim of this page.
