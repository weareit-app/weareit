## Key primary sources

- Popper, K. R. (1959). *The Logic of Scientific Discovery.* Hutchinson (his translation of *Logik der Forschung*, 1934). — Falsifiability as the line between science and non-science.
- Open Science Collaboration (2015). "Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science." *Science* 349(6251): aac4716. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716 — Of 100 repeated studies, 97% of originals were significant, 36% of replications were, and effect sizes were about half.
- Ioannidis, J. P. A. (2005). "Why Most Published Research Findings Are False." *PLoS Medicine* 2(8): e124. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124 — A modeling argument: low power, bias, small effects and many teams make many claimed findings more likely false than true.
- de Moura & Ullrich (2021) on Lean 4 (see `docs/pages/math/research.md`), for what machine-checking adds and doesn't.
