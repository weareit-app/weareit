Where the lenses go blank

Question: scientific inquiry, Spivak's compositional program, and accounts of human agency and behavior change all seem to describe the same loop. Is that overlap structural (each one has real words for the same steps), or only shared vocabulary (they meet on "try, see, update", which a thermostat also does)?

How it works: an episode is a list of neutral events. Each event changes a shared state: what's happening, what the actor believes, how they see it, what they can do, what they plan, what matters to them, and who's involved. Each lens then tries to say what happened. Its verdict is worked out from two rules, not written per event: does the lens have a term for this kind of step, and can it represent every part of the state the step changed? The worse of the two wins. Pain → Dream → Fix and the textbook scientific method ride along as comparisons.

The state follows the candidate actor state X = (L, E, V, A, R) plus the world W from the research-program note: concepts, evidence, values, abilities, and relationships. The episodes are its three casebook cases (pulsars, "I can't find work", and suspected drinking-water contamination), plus bad sleep and a thermostat. They are simplified sketches. A real casebook would use original records and consented observations. A strong decision model lens rides along so the simple rival is not a strawman: anything anticipated in its state space it can say.

Sources: the Oct 2026 deep-research report, "From Pain to Possibility to Inquiry", and the founder's research-program note (2026-10-21).
