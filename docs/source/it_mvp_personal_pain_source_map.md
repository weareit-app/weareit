# It MVP — Personal Pain Source Map

**Purpose:** Maintain the evidence base used to discover, check, and prioritize personal pains for the It MVP. This is a source ledger, not the pain ontology itself.

## Research rule

The MVP onboarding surface should be small and recognizable, while the underlying pain corpus should remain much broader. Sources in this ledger are used to discover candidate problem families, preserve distinctions, estimate prevalence or salience where the source supports that inference, and identify blind spots. A source construct is **not** automatically an onboarding pain.

Keep four things separate:

1. **Source construct** — what the survey, instrument, dataset, complaint taxonomy, or qualitative corpus actually measures or records.
2. **Lived-language gloss** — our plain-language rendering of that construct (for example, “I worry too much”). Unless explicitly documented otherwise, this is not a quotation from the source.
3. **Prevalence evidence** — how common a problem is, only when the source and population support that inference.
4. **Product decision** — whether a pain belongs in Tier 1, Tier 2, or only deeper in progressive disclosure. Frequency alone does not determine this.

Validated psychological/clinical instruments are especially useful for **distinctions and construct coverage**; representative surveys are especially useful for **population prevalence and material conditions**; administrative/complaint data reveal **real-world friction**; qualitative sources are especially useful for **language, edge cases, and missing distinctions**. None should be treated as sufficient alone.

## Source types we should deliberately maintain

| Evidence class | Best use | Main blind spot |
|---|---|---|
| Representative population surveys | Prevalence, subgroup differences, economic and social conditions | Survey questions constrain what can be discovered |
| Validated psych / patient-reported instruments | Fine-grained distinctions; established constructs; symptom/function structure | Often clinicalized; not representative prevalence by itself |
| Time-use and behavioral datasets | What people actually spend time doing; work/care/leisure constraints | Behavior does not directly tell us whether the person experiences it as a pain |
| Administrative / complaint taxonomies | Concrete recurring friction in housing, finance, services, bureaucracy | Selection effects: only reported problems appear |
| Public-health / access frameworks | Social determinants, disability, healthcare access, material constraints | Often optimized for surveillance rather than product language |
| Qualitative communities / first-person corpora | Natural language, unusual cases, causal texture, missing distinctions | Strong selection bias; never use alone for prevalence |
| Relationship / caregiving instruments | Interpersonal and household burdens that broad surveys underresolve | Population- and role-specific |
| Cross-national well-being frameworks | Breadth check against narrow U.S. or clinical framing | Broad indicators may be too coarse for routing |

## Additional source families to audit next

These are **candidate additions**, not yet claimed as incorporated into the recovered corpus:

- **NHIS / National Health Interview Survey** — health status, disability, access, pain, sleep, mental health, social needs.
- **NSDUH / National Survey on Drug Use and Health** — substance use, mental health, treatment need and access.
- **NHANES** — physical health, nutrition, activity, sleep, functional limitations and measured health data.
- **General Social Survey (GSS)** — happiness, social trust, relationships, work, religion, attitudes, social connection.
- **Panel Study of Income Dynamics (PSID)** and related family/economic panels — persistent financial, work, household and caregiving conditions over time.
- **Current Population Survey supplements / ASEC** — work schedules, employment instability, income and household economic conditions.
- **American Community Survey (ACS)** — disability, housing, commuting, household structure, employment, poverty and geographic variation.
- **National Health and Aging Trends Study (NHATS)** — daily function, caregiving, mobility, isolation and aging-related constraints.
- **National Survey of Children's Health (NSCH)** — parenting burden, child health, family resilience, neighborhood and care access.
- **Gallup well-being / workplace datasets** — potentially useful for work engagement, stress and well-being if methodology and accessible question wording support our use.
- **Large-scale diary / experience-sampling research** — useful for moment-to-moment stress, affect, attention and context rather than retrospective self-report alone.
- **Search/query and forum corpora with careful sampling** — useful only for discovery and language; must not be mistaken for population frequency.

## Coverage questions for every future pass

- Are we overrepresenting problems that psychology and medicine already have named scales for?
- Are material and institutional problems represented as well as internal states?
- Are pains of disabled, chronically ill, neurodivergent, poor, rural, immigrant, caregiving, older, formerly incarcerated, and nontraditional-household populations discoverable?
- Do we cover both **states** (“I feel lonely”) and **coordination/logistics failures** (“I have nobody to do things with”)?
- Do we distinguish a cause, a felt problem, a functional limitation, and a downstream consequence rather than collapsing them?
- Does each Tier 1 item route to narrower distinctions without implying that every child applies?
- Where possible, do we know both **how common** the pain is and **how consequential/actionable** it is?
- What pains would never appear because our current sources do not ask about them?

## Relationship to Tier 1

`it_mvp_tier_1_pains_and_dreams.md` is the current presentation-layer shortlist. Its Tier 1/Tier 2 split should be treated as a product decision downstream of this source map, not as a replacement for the evidence corpus. The source map should stay broader than whatever is currently visible in onboarding.

## Maintenance

When adding a source, record at minimum: **source name, evidence type, URL/citation, population, geography, year/wave, constructs or domains contributed, whether it supports prevalence, and known selection/measurement limitations**. When a source produces candidate wording, preserve whether that wording is exact, paraphrased, or generated.
