CORE IDEA
The landing page lets people experience possibilities. The public pages expose the thinking: what is known, conjectured, disputed, unfinished, and available for someone else to continue.

What belongs in this document
This is a companion to the separate v0.1 Product & UX Specification—not a replacement. It captures the working product pages, the nine public pages we have explicitly prioritized, and the other page ideas we discussed. A route on this list is not automatically a launch requirement.

A principle worth preserving
We do not need a universal mathematical kernel before we publish useful knowledge. In the immediate sense, the public atlas itself is a working kernel: put a question or result into the world, explain it, connect it to existing work, show what remains open, and let someone else pick it up. More rigorous machinery can be developed as specific problems demand it.

01  The working product
The interactive MVP remains the priority. These are the pages or surfaces a user actually needs to use It. The public atlas should complement them, not consume the launch schedule.
Route / surface
What it does
/  —  “It can be better.”
One continuous interactive experience. Personal: “What could be better for you?” Societal: “What could be better for us?” Open a pain, explore multiple desired futures, select one or more, then see possible fixes grouped beneath each future. Selected dreams become prominent when a branch collapses. Honest labels separate actions available today from proposed features and speculative societal ideas.
/account or save flow
Optional account after the first meaningful exploration, with quick email-based authentication and persistent choices. “Save your possibilities.” Keep returning participation possible; do not require an account just to try the product.
/support
An appeal and donation form on the same page. Exact leading copy: “It’s free for you—and you’re not the product.” Explain the one-person project, voluntary support, intended lack of ads and data sales, expenses, licensing and eventual community control; show real numbers only.
/help
Tentative lead: “What could you help make possible?” Invite offers of abilities, skills, experience, time, feedback and concrete project help, without promising automated matching.
/unemployment, /loneliness, etc.
Possible topical entry URLs into the same home experience, with an appropriate pain expanded. These are deep links, not wholly separate products or custom-written marketing pages.

AGREED FIRST-RELEASE BOUNDARIES
No personalized story, shareable artifact, invitation/referral loop, or full coordination platform. Accounts remain in scope if straightforward. Real, threshold-protected aggregate interest counts and feature-interest votes are small potential additions, not invented social proof.

02  Priority public pages: why and trust
These nine routes were explicitly singled out as the highest-priority intellectual and explanatory pages. The order below reflects the conversation’s likely launch usefulness, not the intrinsic importance of the underlying subjects.
Route
First version should answer
/why
Why build It at all? Founder’s actual intellectual and practical journey; the human motivation and what the project seeks to change. Not a generic credentials-first bio.
/roadmap
What works now? What is actually under construction? What might come next, what do people want, and how could they help? Distinguish shipped / in progress / proposed and keep it current.
/principles
Commitments on agency, consent, personal information, attention, honesty, openness, independence and hoped-for community ownership. Separate present legal reality from aspirations; explain how principles influence decisions.
/ai
“AI can be helpful. AI can be hurtful. AI can be dangerous.” Explain candidly why AI-generated code and art are used; training-data appropriation, access to knowledge, labor value, benefits, harms, uncertainties, and boundaries. The goal is thoughtful candor, not an all-good/all-bad argument.
/risks
**Risks facing humanity and society**, not merely hazards posed by It. AI, war, bio/pandemic threats, environment, concentrated power, economic displacement, degraded shared knowledge, collective-action failure, and irreversible loss of options. Show evidence, uncertainty and possible responses; address It-specific risks as a subsection or link.

03  Priority public pages: inquiry and possibility
Route
First version should answer
/research
What are the ongoing research programs, actual results, unanswered questions, formal work, empirical investigations, and underlying references? Index work rather than presenting a unified finished theory. Signal maturity, assumptions and independent-review status.
/technology
What are the ambitious technical aims—not merely today’s stack? Fully compositional building blocks; combining heterogeneous information, skills, software, resources, commitments and permissions; discovering new paths; interoperable providers; evolvable capabilities; practical limits of time, memory, communication and information; eventual large-scale use. State what is implemented versus conjectured.
/possibility
An accessible intellectual center: different goals and different situations can lead to more futures than people initially see. Pain → multiple dreams → actions, including the unemployment / financial security / UBI example. Show both personal and collective possibilities without asserting that “possibility” is a solved mathematical primitive.
/future
What could It and the wider world become over longer horizons? Thoughtful possibilities and scenarios, not promises, certainty, or an ungrounded science-fiction roadmap. Where could better knowledge, coordination and technology take us?

06  Make the pages a living research atlas
The public pages can be products of ongoing research, not retrospective promotional material. The objective is to put ideas where other people can evaluate them and keep working on them—even if the founder cannot. A short entry is a useful starting point if it accurately conveys the work and leaves a path for continuation.
The 500-word first-publication template
Part
Include
1  The question
One clear motivating question. Why does it matter for a person, a society, or the project?
2  What is known
Existing knowledge and traceable original sources, including alternative explanations and important previous work.
3  Our present view
A result, hypothesis, design direction, or question. Explicitly label which it is; give the key assumptions.
4  Why it matters
A concrete example or consequence; explain how it could affect decisions, learning, or action.
5  What remains open
Limitations, evidence that is missing, failed paths, unresolved objections and how much remains conjectural.
6  Where to pick this up
What an interested reader could read, challenge, test, formalize, build, or improve next; how to get in touch.

Evidence and attribution
Keep bibliographies and links to primary sources close to the claims they support. Cite other researchers by what they actually published or contributed, not by suggesting endorsement, collaboration, or personal association. Useful starting reference communities and works mentioned include Terence Tao, David Spivak, AI 2027 and related papers; the pages should link precise relevant works as they are written.
Use visible plain-language status labels: “established result,” “machine-checked under these assumptions,” “replicated finding,” “working hypothesis,” “speculative direction,” “open question,” or “planned engineering.” Date substantial revisions and retain prior versions when practical.
Publishing and safety boundaries
Do not publish private data, material likely to facilitate serious harm, or sensitive technical mechanisms before an appropriate safety review. It is fine to describe the motivating question and acknowledge withheld detail. Compilation of a proof is not the same as independent validation; an attractive scenario is not an established prediction.
The first living loop
Publish a note → connect it to people and prior work → invite criticism or contribution → record what changed → make the next step discoverable. This can run manually with Markdown, hyperlinks, a public repository or feedback address, and revision history. No general-purpose graph engine or universal formal kernel is required for version one.
07  A deliberately small rollout
Publishing the atlas is part of building It, but must not delay the functioning first release. A few unusually clear 500-word pages with sources are better than a large set of generic essays or an elaborate publishing system.
When
What to do
Functional MVP
Ship the interactive home, optional save/account, Support, and Help. Prepare a small set of high-quality pain → future → fix branches, direct pain URLs where feasible, and clear crisis resources for relevant content.
First public essays
/why, /roadmap, /principles, /ai, /risks. These answer who is building, what exists, what values guide it, how AI is involved, and what larger problems motivate the work.
Next few essays
/research, /technology, /possibility, /future. These show real research, ambitious compositional engineering, the core idea of reachable futures, and longer-term horizons.
As research develops
/math, /learning, /coordination, /science, /society; then more focused subjects only as worthwhile notes become ready.

The intended longer-term change
The footer begins as a directory of written explanations. Over time, pages can become connected working spaces: cited claims, questions under investigation, related people and institutions, evidence, code or experiments, proposals, requests for expertise, and opportunities to act. The research atlas and the interactive personal/societal explorer could eventually meet in a shared underlying system.
KEEP THE PROJECT GROUNDED
The website can demonstrate intellectual seriousness without relying on conventional credentials by exposing the reasoning, evidence, criticism and unanswered problems. But depth matters more than breadth. A hundred polished essays are less convincing than a handful of transparent, substantive investigations.

Publishing checklist
Can a stranger understand the question and why it matters?
Are facts, findings, hypotheses, preferences and hoped-for capabilities clearly separated?
Do important external claims have verifiable sources and fair attribution?
Can someone else pick up the thread without needing access to private chats?
Have privacy, security and research-safety concerns been considered?
Does the page direct a reader somewhere useful: a product experience, source, question or way to help?

The nine priority pages can be much more than essays without requiring nine new applications. Data discipline
“Dataful” should mean inspectable and informative, not densely decorated. For every chart, number, and status: show its source, definition, unit, population or denominator, date, uncertainty and limitations as appropriate. Distinguish user activity, expressed interest, evidence of usefulness, financial support and completed work. None are interchangeable.
Show aggregate visitor interest only when consent and sufficient group size make disclosure appropriate; do not reveal an individual’s pain, dream, skills or visit history. Choose first-party privacy-respecting analytics where practical. Use plain text and accessible tables as a fallback for all figures.

How to decide whether an interaction deserves to exist
Does interacting reveal a meaningful alternative, a tradeoff, a source, a dependency or a next action that static prose would not communicate as well?
Can the underlying claim be examined, rather than requiring trust in animation or polish?
Can a stranger understand and continue the work without asking the founder to explain it?
Is there a genuinely useful follow-up for a visitor who agrees, disagrees, or has relevant skills?
Can we build it from the existing explorer, /roadmap, /help and a small shared set of content components?
