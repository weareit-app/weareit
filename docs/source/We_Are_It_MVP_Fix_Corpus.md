# We Are It — MVP Fix Corpus

**Status:** Living working document  
**Purpose:** Build the initial corpus of fixes that the We Are It MVP can route people toward from their frustrations, desires, constraints, and observations.

---

## 1. What a “fix” means

A **fix** is a transformation that changes what is possible for a person or group in a situation.

It does not have to be a life hack, recommendation, product, or individual behavior change. A fix can operate at many scales: learning something, changing an environment, acquiring a resource, finding another person, coordinating a group, changing a rule, or building a missing institution or interface.

The MVP should be able to move from:

> **pain → distinction → intervention → try → observation → update**

A fix therefore should not be treated as a universal answer. It is a candidate intervention under particular conditions, followed by observation of what happened.

---

## 2. Design principles

### 2.1 Route toward action, not content

The goal is not merely to explain why a problem exists. The system should help the person find something they can actually try.

### 2.2 Start with the smallest useful action

The first action should generally be cheap, reversible, comprehensible, and possible now. For civic or collective work, the target remains roughly **≤20 minutes** for a first contribution where possible.

### 2.3 Preserve escalation paths

Individual action and systemic action are not competitors.

A useful path may look like:

> **measure → protect yourself → learn → share evidence → find affected people → coordinate → produce public evidence → change infrastructure or policy**

The system should be capable of climbing this ladder when the earlier intervention is inadequate.

### 2.4 Treat refusal and failure as information

“No,” “that won’t work for me,” “I tried that,” and “it didn’t help” should change the route rather than terminate it.

### 2.5 Observe outcomes

Whenever possible, a fix should imply a follow-up observation:

> **Did you try it? What happened? What changed? What did we learn?**

This is what turns a recommendation system into a recursive agency system.

# 4. Personal pain → fix corpus

This is the main working section. Entries should eventually distinguish established interventions, hypotheses worth testing, eligibility conditions, contraindications or risks, cost, effort, evidence quality, and follow-up.

---

## 4.1 “I can’t focus” / “I have brain fog”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Measure relevant aspects of the work/home environment.
- Investigate indoor air quality where relevant.
- Improve particulate filtration where relevant.
- Try a HEPA purifier or appropriate filtration intervention.
- Change the immediate work environment.
- Change the work schedule.
- Reduce environmental distractions.
- Use a focus/task-support tool.
- Test one plausible environmental or behavioral hypothesis at a time.
- Seek appropriate professional evaluation when the pattern warrants it.

### Example route

> **Can’t focus → identify likely constraints → choose one testable hypothesis → make a small intervention → observe whether focus changes → retain, revise, or try another branch**

### Open questions

- What are the highest-value distinctions needed before routing?
- Which interventions are safe enough to suggest without professional evaluation?
- What measurements are cheap enough for the MVP?
- How should subjective improvement be recorded?

---

## 4.2 “I’m exhausted all the time”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Investigate sleep quality.
- Change morning light exposure.
- Change caffeine timing.
- Examine the sleep environment.
- Measure bedroom CO₂ where relevant.
- Reduce noise or other sleep disruption.
- Improve air filtration where relevant.
- Change work scheduling where feasible.
- Investigate childcare or household-load constraints.
- Investigate commuting burden.
- Try appropriate nutrition/exercise changes.
- Seek appropriate clinical screening/evaluation when warranted.

### Important design point

“Exhausted” is an outcome, not a diagnosis. The routing problem is to distinguish among plausible causes and find safe, informative next actions.

## 4.3 “I don’t sleep well”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Identify the dominant sleep constraint.
- Change the sleep environment.
- Establish or modify a wind-down routine.
- Change light exposure.
- Change caffeine timing.
- Reduce noise.
- Investigate temperature/air-quality issues.
- Run one low-cost change as an experiment.
- Observe whether sleep improves.
- Escalate to professional evaluation when appropriate.

### Route shape

> **poor sleep → distinguish likely constraint → low-cost intervention → observe → update**

## 4.4 “I’m lonely” / “I feel isolated”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Find one person.
- Find a nearby activity.
- Find a recurring activity rather than a one-off event.
- Find a group.
- Find a third place.
- Volunteer.
- Create a tiny social commitment.
- Establish a recurring ritual with another person.
- Match around shared interests, needs, or activities.
- Use social-prescribing-style routing where applicable.
- Make an introduction.
- Create a group when the needed group does not exist.

### Escalation path

> **find an interaction → make it recurring → form relationships → connect people to one another → create durable social infrastructure**

## 4.5 “I can’t get myself to do things” / poor follow-through

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Reduce the action to a smaller first step.
- Decompose a goal into manageable tasks.
- Add habit scaffolding.
- Add a reminder or trigger.
- Alter the environment.
- Add accountability.
- Schedule a follow-up.
- Have another person check in.
- Use task tracking.
- Identify whether a missing resource or constraint is actually blocking action.
- Detect stalled actions and reroute.

### Product opportunity

This is a particularly important recursive loop:

> **choose action → attempt → detect completion/refusal/stall → ask why → update model → route again**

## 4.6 “I’m always worried about money”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Build a basic budget/resource snapshot.
- Identify benefits or services for which the person may qualify.
- Reduce a recurring constraint or expense.
- Find a relevant service.
- Identify an income intervention.
- Identify a skill intervention.
- Find mutual aid.
- Build an emergency buffer where feasible.
- Coordinate shared purchasing or resource sharing.
- Identify whether another problem—housing, transportation, debt, healthcare, childcare—is the dominant driver.

The MVP should distinguish **money as the presenting pain** from the particular resource constraint generating it.

## 4.9 “I don’t know what to believe”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

## 4.10 “Pollution / highway pollution is affecting me”

### Candidate fixes already discussed

- Determine likely exposure.
- Measure relevant environmental conditions where feasible.
- Improve indoor filtration.
- Reduce immediate exposure where feasible.
- Learn what is known about the local source.
- Share evidence with affected neighbors.
- Find other affected people.
- Coordinate monitoring.
- Build or contribute to a public dataset.
- Contact responsible institutions.
- Organize around infrastructure or policy change.

### Example escalation

> **measure → protect yourself → learn → share → find others → coordinate → document → change infrastructure/policy**

This is a strong canonical MVP example because a single pain can traverse personal, informational, social, and structural fixes.

# 8. Fix quality criteria

A good MVP fix should ideally be:

1. **Actionable** — there is a concrete next behavior.
2. **Specific** — not merely “exercise more” or “get involved.”
3. **Conditional** — it says when it is relevant.
4. **Small enough to start** — the first action is feasible.
5. **Observable** — we can ask what happened.
6. **Reversible when possible** — especially under uncertainty.
7. **Evidence-aware** — confidence and provenance can eventually be attached.
8. **Constraint-aware** — money, time, ability, location, permissions, and social conditions matter.
9. **Escalable** — failure at the individual level can expose a collective or structural problem.
10. **Composable** — the fix can become one step in a larger plan.

# 9. Corpus-building queue

The immediate task is not to perfect the ontology. It is to accumulate enough high-quality examples that the ontology can be induced and tested against reality.

### Personal frustrations

Add the complete onboarding pain list, then enumerate candidate fixes for every pain.

### Societal frustrations

Add the complete world-frustration list, then identify useful ≤20-minute first actions and escalation paths.

### Cross-cutting work

For each candidate fix:

- identify necessary distinctions;
- identify obvious exclusions/risks;
- attach evidence later;
- estimate cost/time/effort;
- specify the follow-up observation;
- identify personal → social → structural escalation paths;
- note reusable fix primitives.
